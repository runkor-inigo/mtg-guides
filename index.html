Ranges come from the 5 October discussion. Formidable Speaker is core: three or four copies in every version. The deck is built low to the ground: a functional first turn is what lets Cradle raise the ceiling. The exact counts behind that are in the Mana Math further down this page.

Mana base

Nineteen lands with thirteen sources of green for turn one, four Gaea’s Cradle and two Dryad Arbor. The seven fetchlands find Bayou or a Forest, and Forests matter: they are what Quirion Ranger returns, which is why the second Windswept Heath came back over a third Boseiju. Boseiju doubles as removal for a problem land.

Turn-one acceleration

Five or six one-mana dorks, leaning six. A dork on turn one is what makes Cradle and Badgermole Cub matter on turn two. Green Sun’s Zenith for X = 0 costs {G} and fetches Dryad Arbor, a land that is also a creature for Cradle.

Search and selection

Green Sun’s Zenith finds any green creature, Once Upon a Time digs for the piece the opening hand lacks, and Formidable Speaker turns a spare card into a creature from the library. Together they let the deck play the right engine piece for each game instead of hoping to draw it.

Speakers and Untappers

The core of the deck. Formidable Speaker, three or four copies, is the search engine; Quirion Ranger and Wirewood Symbiote, three or four each, untap Cradle and dorks and send Speaker back for another search. How the three work together is explained below.

Mana and board engine

Badgermole Cub turns Gaea’s Cradle into a creature with haste, so the untappers can reuse it, and every creature on the board adds to Cradle. This is where the deck’s explosive turns come from.

Natural Order package

Three or four Natural Order with one Atraxa, the default target that refills the hand, and one or two Craterhoof Behemoth to end the game on the spot. It is the deck’s fastest finish when the board is small; the details are in .

Loop and finish

One Temur Sabertooth turns Speaker, the untappers and Cradle into a repeatable loop with as much mana as needed; Craterhoof Behemoth or a stream of searches finishes. See .

Main-deck flex

The slots that follow the metagame: Marwyn against land destruction, Collector Ouphe against artifacts, Vibrance for mana and a land to channel, Endurance against graveyards, Allosaurus Shepherd against counterspells. Each one replaces part of a package, so it is chosen for the field, not for its own sake. A good flex card passes two tests: it wins games on its own in a few matchups that matter, and it is never the reason a game one is lost in the others.

Speakers and Untappers

These three cards are one package, not three independent slots. What matters is what Quirion Ranger and Wirewood Symbiote do with Formidable Speaker:

  • Symbiote returns Speaker (an Elf) to untap {Q} Gaea’s Cradle. Recasting Speaker gives a new enter trigger, so a discard becomes another creature from the library.
  • Quirion Ranger returns a Forest to untap {Q} a dork or a Cradle that Badgermole Cub has turned into a creature. The returned Forest is also a spare card to discard to Speaker. It cannot return Boseiju or a fetchland that has been sacrificed.
  • Temur Sabertooth resets Symbiote, so its once-per-turn untap {Q} is available again. That is the heart of the .

Main-deck flex slots

A flex slot replaces part of a package, so every choice gains something and costs something. Ask what the slot replaces, which matchup pays for it and whether the deck still keeps enough mana and engine pieces.

Marwyn, the Preserver

Played in the 5 Oct list in Visionary’s slot and out again on 9 October; the first card to bring back if land destruction becomes common. It gives lands with hexproof against Wasteland and other targeted land removal, and a way to get lands back from the graveyard. It does nothing against Blood Moon.

Elvish Visionary

Back in the 9 Oct list, in Marwyn’s slot. An Elf that draws a card: Wirewood Symbiote can return it, and with Temur Sabertooth it powers the draw variant of the loop. Slower than the pieces it competes with, so it stays a flex slot.

Collector Ouphe

A creature-based answer to artifact strategies that Green Sun’s Zenith can find. It is weak where artifacts do not matter.

Vibrance

A 4/4 that, cast with {G}{G}, finds a land (often Boseiju) and gains 2 life; cast with {R}{R}, it deals 3 damage. It can also be evoked for two mana. Japanese Speaker Elves lists play two or three Vibrance as a base.

Endurance

A 3/4 with flash and reach for {1}{G}{G}, or free by exiling a green card from hand. When it enters, a player puts their whole graveyard on the bottom of their library: an answer to graveyard decks that Green Sun’s Zenith can find.

What these probabilities measure

60-card deck, opening seven, no mulligans, no opposing interaction. By T2 you have seen eight cards on the play or nine on the draw.

The two direct lines require one of 13 immediate green sources and one of six actual dorks by T1, then Cradle + Cub + the named payoff by T2. They measure the specific land → dork → Cradle + Cub → payoff sequence. Speaker being cast is not a completed infinite combo or a kill.

These are exact probabilities for those events, computed by combinatorial enumeration of the final draw category and inclusion–exclusion over the required categories in the earlier cards. Ordinary “seen” probabilities use the hypergeometric distribution: P(at least one of K in n) = 1 − C(60−K,n)/C(60,n).

GSZ → Arbor, Once Upon a Time, Quirion routes, other mana sequences and London mulligans are excluded. These line probabilities therefore are not the total probability of a T2 play or win.

Curve uses printed mana value with X = 0 off the stack: GSZ is MV 1, OUAT is MV 2, Vibrance is MV 5. Alternative costs do not change mana value. Lands are excluded from the curve.

Heuristics · decisions before actions

The matchup row shows a proposed test plan for this exact 75; adjust to the opposing build and play/draw. These principles explain decisions; they explain the proposed IN/OUT counts.
SIDEBOARD

Preserve the engine

Keep enough mana and creatures to make Cradle work. Large swaps are deliberate transformations. Speaker belongs to the loop engine group. Start with the matchup row rather than applying a universal number of cuts.

CONTROL

Discard and Choke against blue

Control plans use Thoughtseize and Choke in different quantities; check each row for its exact exchanges. Choke is a green spell, so with Allosaurus Shepherd on the battlefield it cannot be countered: Shepherd first, then Choke, locks a blue deck out of its Islands without giving Force of Will or Daze a target.

ROLE

Who is the beatdown?

Against combo and against midrange or control, Speaker Elves is the beatdown: bring in disruption, cut the slow engine pieces and close the game before their plan arrives. Against tempo and prison decks (Delver, Stompy, Death & Taxes) they are the beatdown: keep up early, trust removal and blockers, and make sure every draw after sideboarding is still live. The role column of the Sideboard map follows this split.

MANA

19 lands · 7 fetches · 2 Boseiju

Two Windswept Heath and one Wooded Foothills: a third Boseiju was tested and left, because fewer Forests in play cost Quirion Ranger value. Boseiju is immediate green mana, but not a Forest for Quirion. Balance playing it as a land against keeping its channel ability. It is legendary, so a second copy on the battlefield adds nothing; extra copies are better kept for channel.

MANA

No Savannah / Massacre exposure

The current list contains no Plains type. Avoid introducing one unnecessarily against Massacre. The current sideboard uses only black and green cards.

SEQUENCING

Count Cradle after paying the bounce

An earthbent Cradle counts itself and has haste. Earthbend does not untap it. When Symbiote returns Speaker, count the remaining creatures before tapping Cradle, then add Cub’s bonus.

COMBO

Play and draw have different hand budgets

For the documented T2 Sabertooth route: play needs Quirion already in hand, two Symbiotes and three discards (including the recovered Forest). Draw uses three Symbiotes and four discards. Do not treat a tutored card as free discard fodder.

COMBO

Speaker’s final ETB is the exception

During the infinite loop, replay Speaker and decline to discard. Only at the finish: leave its ETB on the stack, return Speaker with Sabertooth, then discard that same Speaker to find Hoof. Speaker’s tap ability is unavailable in these T2 lines.

COMBO

Hardcasting Hoof is not always lethal

The earlier Quirion exit can cast Hoof on T2 but leaves the dork and Cradle tapped. With only Hoof attacking, that exact line is a 12/12 trampler. The completed infinite-mana line repeats Hoof ETBs on creatures that stay in play, then untaps animated Cradle for lethal combat. A recast Hoof loses its earlier boosts.

REMOVAL

Assassin’s Trophy and Alpha Deathclaw

Assassin’s Trophy answers any permanent at instant speed but gives the opponent a basic land, so weigh it against their mana. Alpha Deathclaw destroys any permanent when it enters, on a 6/6 with menace and trample. It is black and green, so Green Sun’s Zenith (X = 6), Natural Order and Formidable Speaker can all find it, and Allosaurus Shepherd makes it uncounterable. It costs six to cast: against fast decks it is a Natural Order target more than a spell. Use the matchup row before boarding either.

INTERACTION

Snuff Out and Oracle

Snuff Out only targets nonblack creatures. Removing Oracle in response to its ETB can reduce devotion, but does not stop a zero-card-library Oracle win. Treat it as pressure on specific piles, not a universal answer.

LOCKS

Protect your lands

Marwyn left the main deck on 9 October, so the lands have no hexproof. Against Wasteland: basic Forests first, earthbend Cradle before you expose it (an animated Cradle comes back when it dies), and tap it for mana in response. Vibrance finds a lost land and Boseiju’s channel answers Blood Moon. Marwyn, now in the Maybeboard, is the card to bring back if land destruction becomes common.

RESILIENCE

Vibrance and mana denial

Vibrance is more valuable when the opponent attacks mana. Its green mode can find a land such as Boseiju; its red mode can answer a small creature. Check the mana actually spent, especially under Moon. Use the matchup row for the cut.

RESILIENCE

Sweepers change commitment

Check the row’s warning before adding a fourth or fifth creature. Massacre, Fury, Wrath of the Skies, Terminus, Verdict and Deluge require different play-arounds. Treat the list as a watchlist, not proof of the opponent’s exact 75.

GRAVEYARD

Different hate serves different jobs

Three Leylines supply opening-hand graveyard hate; Thoughtseize can cover transformation plans that bypass graveyards. A Leyline drawn late is not a dead card here: Formidable Speaker discards it to search for a creature. The list has no instant-speed graveyard answer in its sideboard. Do not automatically remove the loop across the whole category.

TESTING

Ideas are separate from the registered list

Underground Mortuary was discussed but not added: surveil helps selection, while entering tapped can delay dork T1 or Speaker T2. Caller of the Claw is also a candidate, not part of the current 75.

GAME PLAN

Faster than Cradle Control

Speaker Elves makes more mana sooner, so it tries the turn-two Natural Order and the explosive turn-two lines more often. Its midrange plan is weaker than Cradle Control’s: do not force a long game while the fast one is open, and check a Cradle Control habit before you borrow it.

EVIDENCE

Prioritize experienced pilots

Weight specialists’ reasoning and your own matchup testing. Repeated copies of one successful list are not independent agreement. Metagame share describes popularity, not matchup win rate.

    Hand requirements and sequencing notes
    • Start with seven cards, without a mulligan. T1 cards must be available on T1; other cards must arrive before use. Tutor targets must remain in the library.
    • The play route requires Quirion in hand. Tutoring for it consumes another search and discard. Without it, the three-Symbiote route requires a fourth discard unavailable from the eight cards seen.
    • Quirion returns Forest/Bayou, not a sacrificed fetchland. The recovered permanent supplies a discard; it is not an extra draw. Boseiju is not a Forest and cannot be returned this way.
    • Stay in the same T2 main phase. Earthbend does not untap Cradle. Speaker is in hand when Cradle taps after each bounce; do not count it. Cub’s extra green is included in all totals.
    • Symbiote can untap the animated Cradle. Speaker has summoning sickness: never use its tap ability in these T2 lines.

    The five setup cards are the first land, dork/GSZ, Cradle, Cub and Speaker. Add two distinct discardable cards: seven cards cover exits A and B. The searches replace each discarded card with the creature you need; returning and recasting Speaker does not supply discard fodder.

For example, open the five setup cards + Hoof + one discardable card, and discard the T2 draw as the second card. Required cards must arrive before their respective play/search.

    Speaker's tap ability is never used here. Symbiote can untap Cradle because it is a creature after earthbend. The five-mana activation happens while Speaker is in hand; count four creatures, not five.

    Game Plans

    Speaker Elves has several routes to a win. A good game starts by recognising which ones the hand can reach, then choosing the one the opponent is least able to stop.

    The routes

    Cradle and Cub

    Gaea’s Cradle plus Badgermole Cub on turn two is the high-ceiling start. It feeds every other route with mana.

    Natural Order

    {2}{G}{G} and a green creature to sacrifice put Craterhoof Behemoth or Atraxa onto the battlefield. Fast, but it relies on one spell resolving.

    Speaker selection

    Each Formidable Speaker trigger turns a discard into the exact creature the turn needs: another untapper, Sabertooth, a bullet or Craterhoof.

    Sabertooth loop

    With enough creatures, Cradle, Symbiotes and Temur Sabertooth, each cycle nets {G}{G} and ends with Craterhoof.

    Setup turn

    Pass turn two with Cradle, Cub, Speaker and Symbiote on the battlefield. On turn three Speaker untaps {Q} Cradle, Symbiote returns it, and the recast Speaker finds Craterhoof. Fewer cards than the loop, one turn of exposure.

    Atraxa

    A Natural Order target that refills the hand instead of ending the game: a better choice when lethal is not available.

    Dinobash (alpha strike)

    Allosaurus Shepherd’s {4}{G}{G}: until end of turn every Elf you control has base power and toughness 5/5 and becomes a Dinosaur. With Cradle mana it turns a wide board of small Elves into lethal without Natural Order or the loop.

    Slower board

    When the explosive routes are blocked, develop creatures, protect the mana and win a longer game.

    With Elvish Visionary the long game can be grindier: Wirewood Symbiote returns Visionary to untap {Q} Cradle, and each recast draws a card. With Temur Sabertooth it draws through the deck (the “Elvish Visionary · draw cards” switch in the ). The 9 Oct list plays one copy again, in place of Marwyn.

    Dinobash math

    Only Elves grow. In this deck that is the one-mana dorks, Quirion Ranger, Allosaurus Shepherd, Formidable Speaker and Elvish Visionary. Wirewood Symbiote, Badgermole Cub, Temur Sabertooth, Collector Ouphe, Dryad Arbor and Craterhoof are not Elves.

    • Damage = 5 × attacking Elves + the power of your other attackers. Elves that entered this turn cannot attack, so count only the ones that can.
    • It sets base power and toughness, so a second activation adds nothing. Counters and other bonuses still apply on top.
    • It stacks with Craterhoof in either order: the new base 5/5 always applies first, then Craterhoof’s +X/+X on top. Activating it after Craterhoof’s trigger has resolved still gives each Elf 5/5 plus X.

    Example: four Elves that can attack and nothing else: 5 × 4 = 20 damage, before blockers.

    Choosing a route

    1. List the routes the hand can reach this turn and next.
    2. For each one, ask the dependency test: what single answer stops it? A dead dork, a counter, a discard spell?
    3. Compare that with the opponent’s likely interaction on their next turns.
    4. Prefer the route with a second route behind it, even if it is a turn slower.

    Pivoting after interaction

    When a piece is answered, the plan changes rather than ends. Natural Order discarded? Speaker can still find the pieces for a longer line. Dork killed? Cradle and Quirion Ranger may rebuild the mana. The worked pivots below cover the answers the deck meets most often. Card rules follow the current Oracle text.

    Your turn-one dork dies

    1. Replace the mana before anything else: a second dork, or Green Sun’s Zenith for X = 0 to fetch Dryad Arbor.
    2. Gaea’s Cradle still counts every creature, Dryad Arbor included, so a turn-two Cradle with one creature already pays for itself.
    3. Badgermole Cub can earthbend a land into a creature: one more body for Cradle, and with Cub every creature tapped for mana adds an extra {G}.
    4. Use bait first next time: the redundant dork before the important pieces (see ).

    Gaea’s Cradle is destroyed

    1. Natural Order still works from lands and dorks. With Badgermole Cub, two dorks tapped for mana make {G}{G}{G}{G}: Natural Order on turn three without Cradle.
    2. Formidable Speaker can discard a spare card to find Vibrance; cast for {G}{G}, Vibrance searches for any land card, a second Gaea’s Cradle included.
    3. Against more land destruction, keep basic Forests in play and Boseiju in hand. If land destruction becomes common, Marwyn, the Preserver (in the Maybeboard) is the card to bring back.

    The earthbent Cradle is killed

    1. Earthbend returns it to the battlefield tapped, and it is no longer a creature.
    2. Quirion Ranger and Wirewood Symbiote can no longer untap it: they target creatures. Formidable Speaker can: {1}, {T}: untap another target permanent.
    3. Before the next big turn, ask whether the board can make the mana without Cradle; if not, a second Cub can earthbend it again.

    Natural Order is countered or discarded

    1. Switch to the Speaker lines: each Speaker trigger turns a spare card into the next piece (Symbiote, Sabertooth, Craterhoof).
    2. Against counters, find Allosaurus Shepherd first: green spells you control can’t be countered, so the next Natural Order or Speaker resolves.
    3. Without the pieces for the loop this turn, take the and kill on turn three.

    Removal on Formidable Speaker

    1. Speaker is an Elf. In response, Wirewood Symbiote can return it to your hand and untap a creature: the removal has no target.
    2. Recast Speaker later for a new enter trigger: another discard, another search.
    3. Without Symbiote, keep the next Speaker until the opponent taps out or has spent the removal.

    A sweeper resolves

    1. Do not refill the board in one turn. Keep one or two creatures in hand, enough to feed Natural Order or a new Speaker.
    2. Gaea’s Cradle and the lands survive most sweepers: one creature next turn already makes Cradle useful again.
    3. The warns which matchups bring sweepers in.

    Blood Moon or Magus of the Moon

    1. Nonbasic lands become Mountains: Cradle, Bayou and Boseiju tap for {R}. Basic Forests and the dorks still make green.
    2. Boseiju’s channel still works from your hand ({1}{G}): it destroys Blood Moon.

    Mulligans

    Judge an opening hand by asking which plan it approaches, whether its mana executes that plan, and how its risks line up against this opponent. There is no universal keep or mull table.

    The common reason to send a hand back is that its mana does not work. A hand with working mana and a lot of it is frequently worth keeping, even without a complete win already in hand. That is a pilot’s judgement, not an instruction to keep every mana-rich seven.

    Six questions for every seven

    1. Initial source. Is there a fetchland, Forest, Boseiju or Bayou? Gaea’s Cradle alone is not a normal first green source.
    2. Turn-one acceleration. Is there a one-mana dork, a Green Sun’s Zenith for Dryad Arbor, or another real equivalent?
    3. Possible paths. Early Cradle and Badgermole Cub, Natural Order, Speaker selection, the Sabertooth loop, Atraxa, or a slower board. See .
    4. Mana without a payoff. If the hand makes lots of mana but has nothing to spend it on yet, count which topdecks turn that mana into action or lethal. It can be a keep; it is not automatically one.
    5. What breaks it. What happens if the first dork dies, the key spell is countered or discarded, a land is attacked or the first search misses? Does the hand have a second route?
    6. Context. Adjust for the matchup, play or draw, and the London mulligan. How likely failure is and what it costs matter as much as raw speed.

    Once Upon a Time and accepted risk

    Missing Cradle and missing the first green source are not the same risk. Missing the first usable source is normally worse, because a game can still develop without Cradle.

    Once Upon a Time can justify a deliberately risky keep when the other six cards are strong. Ask whether it finds the exact missing card, how many other hits or slower lines exist, and how bad a miss is. A keep that needs a first source is more fragile than one that needs Cradle. Throwing back every such hand because the spell might miss sets a risk threshold too conservative for a deck built to use that card. The exact threshold depends on the pilot and the matchup.

    First Turns

    The ideal start is an initial green source, a turn-one accelerator, then Gaea’s Cradle with Badgermole Cub or another engine play on turn two. Natural Order, Speaker, Green Sun’s Zenith or more mana then decides the exact route.

    The deck is built low to the ground for this reason. Cradle raises the ceiling, but a working first turn is what gets the deck there.

    Turn one

    • Play the land that can cast your accelerator now and still supports the turn-two plan. Quirion Ranger needs a Forest to return later, and Boseiju is not a Forest.
    • Green Sun’s Zenith with X = 0 costs {G} and fetches Dryad Arbor, which has summoning sickness: it counts for Cradle on turn two but cannot tap {T} for mana the turn it arrives.

    Natural Order

    Natural Order costs {2}{G}{G} and a green creature, and puts any green creature from your library onto the battlefield. It is the deck’s fastest way to turn a small board into a win, and it is often referred to as “Green Tinker”: like Tinker for artifacts, it trades one permanent for the biggest one in the deck.

    Targets

    Craterhoof Behemoth

    Haste. When it enters, your creatures gain trample and get +X/+X, where X is the number of creatures you control. The finish when the board is wide and can attack.

    Atraxa, Grand Unifier

    Reveals the top ten cards and takes one of each card type into your hand. The choice when lethal is not there and the deck needs fuel.

    The current list plays one of each. The working range is 3–4 Natural Order, 1–2 Craterhoof and 1 Atraxa.

    Targets over the years

    Natural Order was printed in Visions (1997). Each era of the format has pointed it at a different creature:

    • Verdant Force (Tempest, 1997): the first big creature that made the card worth playing (Patrick Chapin, StarCityGames, 2018).
    • Regal Force (Eventide, 2008): a card for each green creature you control. In 2013 the usual pair was one Craterhoof and one Regal Force (Reid Duke, StarCityGames, 2013).
    • Craterhoof Behemoth (Avacyn Restored, 2012): the finisher ever since.
    • Progenitus (Conflux, 2009): protection from everything, the target after sideboarding when the opponent brings in answers to the combo (Reid Duke, 2013; Ross Merriam, StarCityGames, 2014). It is still in 25 of the 914 lists counted in the , mostly Cradle Control.
    • Worldspine Wurm (Return to Ravnica, 2012): a 15/15 second target against Sneak and Show (Ross Merriam, 2014).
    • Archon of Valor’s Reach (Battlebond, 2018; green and white): it names a card type the opponent cannot cast, sorcery against Storm, so it locks out linear combo decks (Newton Hang, 2021–2022).
    • Apex Altisaur (Commander 2019): it fights creature after creature, a one-card answer to a board of small threats. Seen in Cradle Control and Badgermole Elves lists in 2024–2026 (mtgtop8).
    • Atraxa, Grand Unifier (Phyrexia: All Will Be One, 2023): today’s default target, which refills the hand instead of ending the game.

    How much damage does Craterhoof deal?

    Each attacker gets +X/+X, where X is the number of creatures you control when the trigger resolves: Craterhoof itself and an animated Cradle included, attackers or not.

    • Damage = base power of the attackers + attackers × X.
    • When every creature you control attacks, attackers = X, which gives the shortcut X² + base power of the attackers.
    • Creatures that cannot attack (summoning sick, tapped for mana) still raise X, but add no damage themselves. Craterhoof has haste; an earthbent Cradle has haste too.
    • With trample, each blocker only absorbs damage up to its toughness, so subtract roughly the total toughness of their blockers.

    Example: you control 8 creatures but only 5 can attack, with 9 base power between them. Damage = 9 + 5 × 8 = 49. If all 8 could attack with 12 base power: 8² + 12 = 76.

    Setup

    Turn-two Natural Order needs four mana, {2}{G}{G}, by turn two. The guide’s notes show three routes. The ’s “Turn-2 Natural Order” mode plays all three step by step: its card switches pick the route.

    • Dork or Arbor, Cub and Cradle: Cradle sees three creatures and taps {T} for {G}{G}{G}; Cub adds one more {G}.
    • Dork or Arbor, Quirion Ranger and Cradle: Quirion returns the Forest to untap {Q} the dork for a second activation.
    • Turn-one Quirion, then Cub: Quirion untaps {Q} the animated Cradle, with no turn-one dork needed.

    The creature you sacrifice is gone for the rest of the turn: one fewer for Cradle and one fewer for Craterhoof’s +X/+X. Count it before choosing what to sacrifice.

    Protection and failure

    • Counters: Allosaurus Shepherd makes your green spells impossible to counter, Natural Order included.
    • Discard: a hand built only around Natural Order loses everything to one discard spell. Keep a second route.
    • Karakas and exile: Atraxa is legendary, and exile effects answer whichever creature arrives. Revisit Natural Order and Atraxa against heavy exile and control.
    • Craterhoof is not always lethal. If the line leaves your creatures tapped or summoning sick, only some of them can attack. A turn-two Natural Order off Cub and Cradle sees three creatures, and only the hasty Craterhoof can attack: 8 damage.

    Speaker Loop

    The Speaker engine wins in two ways. The turn-two kill starts an infinite loop on turn two and ends with Craterhoof. The setup turn passes with Gaea’s Cradle and the engine on the battlefield, then untaps and kills on turn three. In both, what matters most is what each Speaker trigger searches for, and when.

    Before Sabertooth arrives, each recast Speaker is one more trigger: discard a card that no longer helps and search the next piece, usually another Symbiote.     Build the board, or just make mana?

    Each time a Symbiote returns an Elf, choose by what the turn still needs.

    You still need pieces: return Speaker

    Speaker, Symbiote, return Speaker; Speaker, Symbiote, return Speaker… until Speaker finds Sabertooth. Then loop and kill. Every trigger finds a creature that stays on the battlefield, so the board and Cradle’s mana grow each time.

    You already have the kill: return a cheap Elf

    When the finish is already in hand, for example Craterhoof itself ({5}{G}{G}{G}) or a Green Sun’s Zenith for it ({8}{G}), you only need mana. Return a one-mana Elf (a dork or Quirion) instead of Speaker: it costs {G} to recast instead of {2}{G}. Seven creatures still untap Cradle for eight mana, so the cycle nets {G}{G}{G}{G} instead of {G}{G}.

    With unlimited loops both reach the same result, but looping a one-mana Elf gets to the critical amount of mana in fewer steps. Pilots do this in real games.

    The finish

    1. Cast Speaker one last time and leave its trigger on the stack.
    2. In response, pay {1}{G} to Sabertooth and return Speaker to your hand.
    3. Resolve the trigger: discard that same Speaker and find Craterhoof Behemoth.
    4. Cast Craterhoof. With enough mana, return and recast it: each new Craterhoof is a new object and its bonus stacks on the creatures that stay. Then untap {Q} Cradle and attack.

    Which route?

    Turn-two kill

    Wins before the opponent untaps. Needs more cards: on the play, Quirion Ranger already in hand and three discards; on the draw, three Symbiotes and four discards. Every Speaker trigger must find the next piece in order.

    Setup turn

    Needs only the turn-two setup and one discard, because Craterhoof is searched on turn three. The engine sits on the battlefield for one opposing turn: weigh their sweepers, removal and land destruction first.

    When the last piece is missing

    Sometimes the mana or the discards run out before the last piece of the loop, for example Temur Sabertooth. The turn is not wasted: the mana and the Speaker triggers you already made still pay for spells. Turn it into a setup turn and spend what is left on one of two plans.

    Protect the next turn

    Use a Speaker trigger on a piece against the opponent’s plan instead of the missing combo piece: Collector Ouphe against artifacts, Alpha Deathclaw (after sideboarding) against any permanent, Allosaurus Shepherd against counters, or Vibrance to find Boseiju: evoked for {G}{G}, it puts the land into your hand. Channel Boseiju ({1}{G}, discard it; {1} less for each legendary creature you control, such as Atraxa) on an artifact, enchantment or nonbasic land. The opponent may then fetch a land with a basic land type. Breaking a Tron land or Planar Nexus on turn two this way is a very strong play. Then kill on turn three as in Route 2: once their key piece is gone, that return turn usually wins.

    Do the most damage

    When no kill is possible this turn or the next, use all the mana for damage now, for example Allosaurus Shepherd’s {4}{G}{G} on a wide board of Elves (see Dinobash in ).

    Which plan to choose depends on two things: the most mana you can reach this turn, and the deck across the table. See .

    Common mistakes

    • Counting Speaker for Cradle while it is in your hand after a bounce.
    • Discarding to Speaker during the loop: decline the discard until the finish.
    • Using Speaker’s {1}, {T} untap ability in the turn-two lines: it has summoning sickness. On the setup turn it is ready.
    • Tapping Cradle for mana before the setup turn’s untaps: count the creatures still on the battlefield each time.
    • Expecting a recast Craterhoof to keep its own earlier boosts: it is a new object.
    • Stopping the loop without a chosen number of repetitions.

    Rules that run through every window

    • Earthbend does not make Cradle easy to destroy. Cub’s earthbend turns Cradle into a 1/1 creature land. If it dies or is exiled, it returns to the battlefield tapped, as a plain land. Removal on an animated Cradle costs its mana this turn, not the card.
    • Only Speaker untaps a plain Cradle. Wirewood Symbiote and Quirion Ranger untap target creature. Once Cradle is no longer a creature, only Formidable Speaker’s {1}, {T} can untap it. Cradle no longer counts itself, and Cub adds no extra {G} when it taps, because Cub’s bonus is for creatures.
    • Allosaurus Shepherd stops counters, nothing else. With Shepherd on the battlefield, your green spells can’t be countered: Cub, Speaker, Natural Order, Sabertooth and Craterhoof included. It does not stop Stifle effects, removal or discard.
    • Your lands have no hexproof. Marwyn left the main deck on 9 October, so an animated Cradle can be targeted like any creature. Tap it for mana in response whenever you can.
    • Mana empties between steps and phases. If an untapped Cradle is about to be destroyed or removed, tap it for mana in response; that mana stays until the end of the phase.

    Counters and Stifle

    Who has them: the blue decks, marked by a red Counters light on their matchup card (Dimir Tempo, Izzet Delver, Doomsday, Sneak and Show, Azorius Tempo). Force of Will and Daze are the usual ones; Stifle effects counter abilities instead of spells.

    Where it hurts most

    1. Stops the line Natural Order. The card, the sacrificed creature and four mana in one answer.
    2. Stops the line Temur Sabertooth before the loop, or the Speaker whose search the line needs.
    3. Slows it Badgermole Cub on turn two, often with Daze while the deck is tapped out.
    4. Stops the line Stifle on a single trigger the turn depends on: earthbend, a Speaker search or Craterhoof.

    The deck’s fallback

    • Allosaurus Shepherd first. It can’t be countered, and while it stays on the battlefield your green spells can’t be countered either. A counter still targets them; it just does nothing. Green Sun’s Zenith for X = 1 or a Speaker search finds it.
    • Pay for Daze. Leave {1} spare on the key turn; Cradle usually makes that cheap.
    • Many cheap spells over one big one. Without Shepherd, a Speaker line spreads the risk; Natural Order puts everything on one spell.
    • Sideboard: Thoughtseize takes Force of Will before the key turn; Choke keeps their Islands tapped (Underground Sea and Volcanic Island are Islands).

    Mostly wasted: a Force of Will on a one-mana dork (two of their cards for one of yours), and any counter once Shepherd has resolved. What Shepherd does not cover: spells that aren’t green (Thoughtseize, Snuff Out and Leyline of the Void; Assassin’s Trophy and Alpha Deathclaw are green, so they are covered), Stifle effects, and Mindbreak Trap, which exiles spells rather than countering them. A loop turn easily casts the three spells that make Trap free, though Trap is rare in the current sideboards.

    Spot removal

    Who has it: almost everyone. Fatal Push and Orcish Bowmasters appear in about half the Dimir Tempo and Doomsday sideboards; white decks bring Swords to Plowshares, and Death and Taxes adds Karakas.

    Where it hurts most

    1. Stops the line The animated Cradle during the loop, or in your upkeep before the setup-turn kill. It is a colorless 1/1 with mana value 0: Fatal Push, Lightning Bolt and one Bowmasters ping all kill it.
    2. Stops the line Badgermole Cub in the loop: each cycle drops to no gain.
    3. Stops the line Shepherd, when they also hold a counter for the next spell.
    4. Stops the line Speaker on the board passed for the setup turn.

    The deck’s fallback

    • Save the creature in response. Wirewood Symbiote returns an Elf (dorks, Quirion, Shepherd, Speaker, Elvish Visionary) once a turn; Temur Sabertooth returns any creature for {1}{G}. A returned Speaker searches again when you recast it.
    • Redundancy. Six dorks, two Dryad Arbor, four Cub and four Speaker: one removal spell is usually one card for one card.
    • Sideboard: Alpha Deathclaw (Speaker, Green Sun’s Zenith and Natural Order can find it) and Assassin’s Trophy kill any creature; Snuff Out costs 4 life with Bayou out, but can’t target a black creature such as Bowmasters.

    Orcish Bowmasters is mostly its enter trigger here: Speaker, Once Upon a Time and Atraxa put cards into your hand without drawing them, so the deck rarely triggers it twice. That ping still kills any 1-toughness creature or the animated Cradle, and it arrives with flash. Karakas and bounce are a special case: the animated Cradle is a legendary creature, so Karakas can target it, and because it goes to your hand rather than dying it does not come back on its own. You need another land drop to replay it.

    Mostly wasted: a dork after the mana is already made, Craterhoof once its trigger is on the stack, and a destroy effect on Sabertooth after its first activation that turn (it is indestructible).

    Wasteland and land answers

    Who has them: the decks with a red Wasteland light on their matchup card. Surgical Extraction appears in roughly half or more of the Izzet Delver, Azorius Tempo, Boros Energy and Death and Taxes sideboards, and Magus of the Moon in most Sneak and Show sideboards.

    Where it hurts most

    1. Stops the line Wasteland on Cradle before it is animated, or in response to the earthbend trigger. Cradle is gone for good (part 1, turn two).
    2. Stops the line Surgical Extraction on that Cradle in your graveyard: it exiles every other Cradle from your hand and library. The Cradle routes are off for the game.
    3. Stops the line Blood Moon or Magus of the Moon. Cradle, Bayou, Boseiju and the fetchlands become Mountains; only basic Forests and the dorks make green.
    4. Slows it Wasteland on the only green land on turn one.

    The deck’s fallback

    • Vibrance. Cast for {G}{G}, it searches for a land card, a second Gaea’s Cradle included. Marwyn, the Preserver (hexproof lands, and {2} to return a land from the graveyard) left the main deck on 9 October: bring her back if land destruction becomes common.
    • Vibrance finds a land. Evoked for {G}{G}, it searches for any land card (another Cradle, or a basic Forest under Moon) and gains 2 life. Speaker can find it.
    • Basic Forests. Fetch them when Wasteland is likely. Quirion Ranger can return a targeted Forest or Bayou to your hand in response.
    • Boseiju’s channel destroys Blood Moon from your hand, even while Moon is out, for {1}{G}. Assassin’s Trophy needs {B}, and under Moon Bayou makes only {R}.

    Mostly wasted: Wasteland on an animated Cradle (it returns tapped, so it costs only this turn’s mana) and on a fetchland you can crack in response.

    Sweepers

    Who has them: the decks with a red Sweepers light. In the current sideboards: Massacre in most Dimir Tempo lists, Wrath of the Skies in Death and Taxes and Azorius Tempo, Brotherhood’s End in Sneak and Show, Eldrazi Confluence in Eldrazi, and Drop of Honey in about half of the Lands lists. Massacre is not free here: the deck has no Plains.

    Where it hurts most

    1. Stops the line On their turn, after you pass for the setup turn. The whole engine is on the battlefield; Cradle returns tapped as a plain land.
    2. Slows it After you commit a second wave of creatures to rebuild a board.
    3. Slows it Drop of Honey destroys the creature with the least power in each of their upkeeps: slow, but it never stops.

    The deck’s fallback

    • Know what survives. Massacre (−2/−2) leaves Speaker (2/4) and Sabertooth (4/3); Brotherhood’s End (3 damage) leaves Speaker; Wrath of the Skies must pay 3 energy to reach Speaker and 4 for Sabertooth. Toxic Deluge kills whatever they pay life for.
    • Hold something back. A Speaker or Symbiote in hand rebuilds faster than any card on the battlefield; Cradle refills the mana from two or three creatures.
    • Respond with Sabertooth. Its ability works on their turn: return creatures to your hand for {1}{G} each. Once activated, Sabertooth itself is indestructible, so it survives damage and destroy sweepers but not −X/−X.
    • Choose the turn-two kill over the setup turn when they show sweepers. Sideboard: Thoughtseize takes the sweeper first.

    Mostly wasted: a sweeper on a board of two or three creatures while you hold more, and a sorcery-speed sweeper after the turn-two kill.

    Graveyard and “enter” hate

    Who has it: Containment Priest is in most Azorius Tempo, Boros Energy and Death and Taxes sideboards; Grafdigger’s Cage in some Sneak and Show lists; Leyline of the Void in Eldrazi; Endurance in Lands.

    Where it hurts most

    1. Stops the line Containment Priest with flash in response to Natural Order or Green Sun’s Zenith: the creature wasn’t cast, so it is exiled instead of entering. Dryad Arbor is exiled too, even when you play it as your land.
    2. Stops the line Grafdigger’s Cage before your big turn: creatures can’t enter from the library, so Natural Order and Green Sun’s Zenith find nothing, Arbor included.
    3. Slows it Surgical Extraction on a Cradle or a Natural Order that reached your graveyard.

    The deck’s fallback

    • Switch to Speaker routes. Speaker puts creatures into your hand and you cast them, so neither Priest nor Cage touches the loop, the setup turn, a hard-cast Craterhoof or Dinobash.
    • Remove the hate piece. Boseiju’s channel, Assassin’s Trophy and Alpha Deathclaw destroy Cage. Collector Ouphe does not stop it, because Cage has no activated ability. Snuff Out, Assassin’s Trophy and a cast Alpha Deathclaw kill Priest (Priest exiles a Deathclaw that Natural Order puts onto the battlefield).
    • Play around flash. Against white decks with open mana, cast Speaker before risking Natural Order.

    Mostly wasted: Leyline of the Void, Endurance and other graveyard removal. The deck does not use its graveyard, so they cost it nothing.

    The fallback plan

    When the fast routes are answered, the deck does not need a new plan: it runs the same engine one turn slower. In order of preference:

    1. The second route. Natural Order answered? Speaker lines remain. Cradle gone? Natural Order still works off lands and dorks. This is why prefers hands with two routes.
    2. Speaker as a toolbox. Each Speaker discards a spare card for the creature that solves the problem: Shepherd against counters, Vibrance against land answers, Collector Ouphe against artifacts with activated abilities, and Alpha Deathclaw against any permanent after sideboarding. Symbiote and Sabertooth return Speaker for another search.
    3. Dinobash. Shepherd’s {4}{G}{G} is an ability, not a spell: counters can’t answer it, though Stifle can. With Cradle mana it turns Elves that can attack into 5/5s; see .
    4. Atraxa. When Natural Order resolves but Craterhoof is not lethal, Atraxa refills the hand for the next turn.
    5. A slower board. Speaker blocks well as a 2/4, every creature makes Cradle bigger, and the deck can wait a turn for the opponent to tap out.

    Sequencing

    Speaker Elves grows exponentially: every creature adds mana to Cradle, and every Speaker trigger adds a creature. One play in the wrong order on turn one or two costs more here than in most decks, and it is where most games are lost. These habits are adapted to this list from Newton Hang’s sequencing primers for Cradle decks.

    When a habit and a concrete line disagree, count the line. The shows the exact turn-two lines; shows what each answer from the opponent does to them.

    Before your first turn

    • Do not pass priority automatically. It tells the opponent what you are not holding.
    • If a discard spell would take the land you need, cast Once Upon a Time in response to find one.
    • Do not cast Once Upon a Time only to play around Daze: seeing your next draw and keeping your hand hidden matter more.

    Turn one

    Which land

    • When in doubt, play a basic Forest over a fetchland, and a Forest over Bayou. Forests are what Quirion Ranger returns, and a basic is safe from Wasteland.
    • Against Blood Moon decks, fetch early for basics: two basic Forests still cast Natural Order under Moon.
    • Against “you can’t search” effects (Opposition Agent), crack fetchlands early, before they land.

    Fetchland or Once Upon a Time

    • When in doubt, cast Once Upon a Time first: it may find the land itself.
    • When you do not need a land, crack the fetchland first, so Once Upon a Time does not show you the land you were about to fetch, and the library is thinner for the five cards you look at.
    • Fetch first, too, when Once Upon a Time is digging for one specific creature, such as Badgermole Cub or Allosaurus Shepherd.

    Which accelerator

    • When in doubt, make mana: a one-mana dork, or Green Sun’s Zenith for X = 0 to fetch Dryad Arbor.
    • Against a spell combo deck with Force of Will, Green Sun’s Zenith first (it is the play they most want to counter), Allosaurus Shepherd next.
    • Keep Green Sun’s Zenith when there is nothing to spend the mana on next turn: later it can find Collector Ouphe, Elvish Visionary or Formidable Speaker.

    Lands that do not make mana now

    • Play Dryad Arbor after your spells: it cannot tap for mana the turn it arrives.
    • The exception is a turn where Cradle needs one more creature: then Arbor goes first.

    Turn two

    Against counterspells

    • Lead with Allosaurus Shepherd: every green spell after it, Natural Order and Choke included, cannot be countered.
    • With an unimportant creature in hand, test for Force of Will with it before you commit Badgermole Cub, Speaker or Natural Order.

    Against Daze and Wasteland

    • Make your land drop before the key spell when you would otherwise have only two mana.
    • Crack a fetchland before the key spell too: a fetchland in play is a Wasteland target that also feeds Daze.

    Against removal

    • When in doubt, make the most mana you can.
    • Bait with the least important creature: a second one-mana dork first, then Quirion Ranger, then Wirewood Symbiote. Keep Badgermole Cub and Formidable Speaker for when the removal has been spent.
    • Baiting only pays when the turn after can punish an opponent who kept the removal: a hand with Cradle and Cub, or Natural Order.

    Against combo

    • You are the beatdown (see ): one or two pieces of disruption, then the fastest kill. Do not try to race a deck that wins a full turn earlier.
    • After sideboarding, commit creatures to the board aggressively: Natural Order is the quickest way to end the game once the disruption has landed.

    The Speaker turn

    On the turn the engine goes off, order the casts to lose as little as possible to a single answer:

    • Against removal: redundant dork, other dorks, Quirion Ranger, Wirewood Symbiote, then Formidable Speaker and its search.
    • Against counterspells: Allosaurus Shepherd first, then the same order.
    • Count Cradle after paying for each bounce, and add Badgermole Cub’s extra {G} for every creature that taps for mana. The exact loop is in .

    Deck Origins

    Speaker Elves sits at the end of a long line of green creature decks built around Gaea’s Cradle. This page is a short, sourced orientation. The rest of the guide does not inherit these older packages.

    From Berlin to Speaker

    1. 2008

      Pro Tour Berlin: the Elves engine

      The archetype was born in Extended. Heritage Druid and Nettle Sentinel combined with Glimpse of Nature so that every Elf cast added mana and drew a card. Six copies of the deck reached the top 8, and Luis Scott-Vargas won the event. Some versions could win as early as turn two.

    2. 2008–12

      Glimpse Elves in Legacy

      The engine was quickly ported to Legacy, where it gained Gaea’s Cradle, Quirion Ranger and Natural Order. Mirror Entity became the usual kill. It stayed on the fringe of the metagame: it leaned heavily on Glimpse of Nature and struggled once it was short of resources.

      Results and people: one of the earliest Legacy results on record is Brad Herwy’s Aggro Elves, top 4 at the GenCon 2008 Legacy Championship (160 players, 15 August 2008). Chris Andersen was the best-known champion of the first combo version (Ross Merriam, StarCityGames, 2014).

    3. 2013

      Natural Order for Craterhoof

      Craterhoof Behemoth (Avacyn Restored, 2012) gave Natural Order a one-card finish. Matt Nass reached the top 8 of Grand Prix Denver in the first week of 2013 with a Natural Order and Craterhoof list, and Deathrite Shaman (since banned in Legacy) added extra one-mana acceleration. By 2014 Elves was one of the most played and successful decks on the StarCityGames Open Series.

      Results and people: Reid Duke (MTGO: reiderrabbit; @ReidDuke), who calls Elves his favourite Legacy deck, won the SCG Legacy Open Philadelphia with Elves in September 2013. Andrew Cuneo added Worldspine Wurm for Grand Prix Washington DC, and Ross Merriam, who played the deck from the SCG Invitational in Los Angeles to Grand Prix New Jersey, wrote its standard guide (Ross Merriam, StarCityGames, 2014). In Europe Julian Knab (MTGO: Julian23; @itsJulian23), host of Everyday Eternal, the oldest Legacy podcast, was the deck’s great specialist: he won the Bazaar of Moxen 8 Legacy main event in Paris with Elves (695 players, 3 November 2013), then MKM Series Milan 2016 and MKM Series Hamburg 2018, and reached the top 8 of the Legacy European Championship 2017: twelve Elves top finishes from 2013 to 2018 (mtgtop8). Later Grand Prix results: Juan Félix Flury, top 8 at Grand Prix Paris 2014; Lukas Müller, 8-0 on day one of Grand Prix Birmingham 2018.

    4. 2019–22

      Combo Elves with Glimpse and Reclaimer

      Elvish Reclaimer (Core Set 2020, July 2019) gave Glimpse Elves a creature that fetches Gaea’s Cradle or a utility land, and Once Upon a Time (October 2019) added free selection. This was combo Elves at its most played.

      How Cradle Control began: Newton Hang (@hello_newton) challenged Nettle Sentinel, which needed Heritage Druid or Birchlore Rangers to matter, and argued for Elvish Reclaimer because it finds Gaea’s Cradle reliably. Most of the Elves Discord dismissed it, so he started his own Discord to develop the idea. At Eternal Weekend 2021, Jörg Heinrich (EronRelentless), Newton (as xWhale) and Peter van der Ham (Maraxus_of_NL) all reached the top 8 with the Reclaimer Glimpse list (Curran Delahanty, Cradle Control: An Overview).

      Published results: Glimpse of Nature in at least 274 Elves lists a year in 2021 and 2022; Reclaimer in 58–82 Elves lists every quarter of 2022.

      Results and people: MTGGoldfish ranked Elves the fourth-best Legacy deck of 2020, split into an Elvish Reclaimer and Once Upon a Time build and a Nettle Sentinel build. At the Legacy Challenge of 5 December 2020 Comeback won with Nettle Sentinel and Eron Relentless (Jörg Heinrich) was second with Reclaimer. The same article names Newton Hang and Julian Knab among the deck’s most focused pilots (Joe Dyer, MTGGoldfish, 9 December 2020).

    5. 2022–23

      Fiend Artisan Elves

      Fiend Artisan (Ikoria, 2020) sacrifices a creature to put any creature with the right mana value from the library onto the battlefield, so Elves gained another tutor for Craterhoof and its bullets. Some lists were called Artisan Elves.

      Why it came in: the rule Newton Hang and Curran Delahanty followed was “play cards that don’t require other cards to be good”. Heritage Druid and Glimpse of Nature were cut next, and Fiend Artisan, overlooked since Ikoria, took their place after an “emergency stream”. Curran won a Legacy Challenge with it in November 2022 (Curran Delahanty, Cradle Control: An Overview).

      Published results: 29 Elves lists with Fiend Artisan in Q3 2022 and 57 in Q4 2022, falling to 14 in Q1 2023.

      Results and people: at the Legacy Challenge of 30 October 2022 Elves finished 3rd (JHK) and 6th (Testacular, Curran Delahanty). Earlier that year the Cradle Control pilots were already there: Testacular was 2nd and EronRelentless 3rd in MTGO Legacy Challengers on 17 and 24 July 2022 (TCDecks). Reid Duke reached the final of a Legacy Challenge on stream that December and explained his list in Legacy Elves (Ultimate Guard, January 2023).

    6. Jun 2023

      Orcish Bowmasters

      Orcish Bowmasters (The Lord of the Rings, 23 June 2023) deals damage whenever an opponent draws extra cards, which is exactly what Glimpse of Nature does. Combo Elves never recovered. Specialists kept Glimpse at large paper events, but it stopped being popular.

      Published results: Glimpse Elves on MTGO fell from 30 lists in Q2 2023 to 3 in Q3 2023; all Glimpse results fell from 176 in 2023 to 22 in 2024. Since July 2023, 66 of its 98 finishes came from paper events, including six lists at Eternal Weekend Europe 2023, NA Legacy Champs 2023 and the Asia Legacy Championships of 2023–2025.

    7. 2023–24

      Cradle Control: the Reclaimer era

      Golgari Cradle Control kept Gaea’s Cradle but played a longer, grinding game. Elvish Reclaimer and Crop Rotation found Cradle and utility lands, and Fiend Artisan tutored threats. Glimpse of Nature had been cut before the deck took the name, though its ancestor was the Reclaimer Glimpse list. Endurance became a playset, Symbiotes and Visionaries left and Noble and Ignoble Hierarch replaced the one-mana Elves. By April 2024 Fiend Artisan “felt like it had finally been beaten” (Curran Delahanty, Cradle Control: An Overview).

      Published results: Cradle Control appears in Q4 2023, with Fiend Artisan in 84 lists and Elvish Reclaimer in 64 that quarter.

      Pilots who shaped Cradle Control: Newton Hang (MTGO: hellonewton; @hello_newton), Curran Delahanty (MTGO: Testacular), David Schittinger (MTGO: dssit; @Dsitt7) and Jörg Heinrich (MTGO: EronRelentless; @Eron_Relentless).

    8. 2024–26

      Cradle Control with Wight of the Reliquary

      Wight of the Reliquary (Modern Horizons 3, June 2024) sacrifices another creature to fetch any land, Cradle included, and grows with creature cards in the graveyard. It gradually replaced Elvish Reclaimer. Finishers such as Atraxa, Grand Unifier close the game.

      Along the way, from Curran Delahanty, Cradle Control: An Overview: Modern Horizons 3 also brought Springheart Nantuko and, from its Commander decks, Talon Gates of Madara. In October 2024 Sylvan Safekeeper protected Wight (“if we untap with Wight, we win”, in Newton Hang’s words). Newton then won a showcase with Scythecat Cub, which replaced Craterhoof for a while; after the bans of Troll of Khazad-dûm and Sowing Mycospawn, Keen-Eyed Curator took the Cub’s place.

      Published results: at least 134 Cradle Control lists with Wight in the second half of 2024. From May to October 2026, 69 Cradle Control lists play Wight and only 9 still play Reclaimer.

    9. Nov 2025

      Badgermole Cub: two answers

      Badgermole Cub (Avatar: The Last Airbender, 21 November 2025) adds green whenever a creature taps for mana and can turn Cradle into a hasty creature. Cradle Control adopted it at once. Elves tried the spiritual successor of the combo approach: mana dorks, Cub and Fiend Artisan racing to a fast Craterhoof. Those lists did not succeed.

      The same month the 10 November 2025 bans removed Entomb and Nadu. Curran Delahanty’s overview singles out runkor’s Badgermole Cub list (Wight, Springheart Nantuko, three Wasteland, Thoughtseize in the main deck) and its Quirion Ranger: “untap target creature” becomes very strong once Gaea’s Cradle is a creature, the idea Speaker Elves builds its engine on. Newton’s own list adopted Quirion and Springheart soon after (Curran Delahanty, Cradle Control: An Overview).

      Published results: 108 Cradle Control lists with Cub in November and December 2025. The dorks, Cub and Fiend Artisan Elves lists appear at Eternal Weekend Europe 2025 (two lists) and only once more afterwards.

    10. 2026

      Speaker Elves

      Formidable Speaker (Lorwyn Eclipsed, January 2026) turns a discard into a creature tutor that Wirewood Symbiote and Temur Sabertooth can reuse. Rediscovered in Japan by Shimoizumi Ryoichi (@ryo_sll) and Takagi Yuki (@taka87z3), Speaker Elves is the natural heir to the combo approach: selection and mana instead of Glimpse’s draw chains, so Bowmasters punishes it far less.

    What every Cradle deck shares

    Creature count is mana

    Gaea’s Cradle adds {G} for each creature you control, so every one of these decks is dense with cheap creatures. Each extra body raises the ceiling of the next turn.

    Untapping Cradle

    Quirion Ranger and Wirewood Symbiote untap {Q} a creature once per turn. Pointed at a mana creature, or at a Cradle that has become a creature, they turn one land into several activations.

    Finding Cradle

    Each generation found its own way to reach Cradle: Once Upon a Time, Elvish Reclaimer, Crop Rotation, Wight of the Reliquary. A Cradle deck that cannot find its Cradle plays a much smaller game.

    Where they differ

    Glimpse Elves turned mana into cards. Natural Order lists turned four mana into Craterhoof. Cradle Control turns land access into a long game. Speaker Elves turns a discard into the exact creature the turn needs.

    That changes how each one plays. Speaker Elves makes more mana, sooner, so it tries the turn-two Natural Order and the other explosive turn-two lines more often than Cradle Control. Its midrange plan is weaker: when the game goes long, Cradle Control’s land engine grinds better, so Speaker Elves should not force a slow game while the fast one is still open. Not every Cradle Control habit transfers.
